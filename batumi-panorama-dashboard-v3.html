The real story

The root causes

This is the level that matters in your meeting. Not symptoms. Causes.
The project still photographs well.
That is exactly why the problem is dangerous. Pretty images buy attention. But once buyers try to verify the truth, the story fractures.
Root cause 1

No single source of truth

Public sources disagree on completion status, inventory, finish, and delivery. The market sees multiple realities at once.

Root cause 2

Silence where proof should be

There is not enough fresh, dated evidence showing what exists, what operates, what is sold, and what is occupied today.

Root cause 3

Sales promises outran execution proof

ROI, payback, and management comfort were marketed more aggressively than the current operational reality was documented.

Root cause 4

The secondary market took over the narrative

Resale and unfinished-inventory style listings now explain the project to the public more powerfully than the developer does.

Meeting mode

The line to take in the room

Don't let them drag you into soft marketing talk. Force them onto reality.
"This is not mainly a marketing problem. It is a trust problem created by conflicting public realities."
1. What is the exact current status today — completed, partially completed, stalled, or relaunching?
2. Why do public sources conflict on completion timing, finish level, and total unit count?
3. Which amenities are physically operational right now, not planned?
4. Where is the dated proof: current video, occupancy, operator, rent, resident activity?
5. If the project is healthy, why is the public story still mostly being told by stale aggregator pages?
6. What is the 12-month recovery plan, with milestones and named accountability?
