Core diagnosis

The issue is not weak aesthetics. It is trust collapse through inconsistent narratives.

Batumi Panorama does not read like a live, well-managed project. It reads like a fragmented promise with no clear current owner of the truth. Public sources conflict on completion, unit count, finish level, and what is actually operational. For outside buyers, that confusion becomes the story — and the market fills silence with assumptions of delay, liquidity stress, or execution failure.

Public-relations failure

This is mostly a silence problem.
  • No strong current official narrative. Third-party portals are still carrying the story.
  • Weak social proof. Public social footprint appears thin, stale, or low-engagement.
  • No crisis framing. There is no visible explanation for delays, partial delivery, or status ambiguity.
  • Silence invites the worst inference. In distressed real estate, silence gets interpreted as liquidity or execution trouble.

Marketing failure

The concept is coherent. The proof is not.
  • Positioning is not the problem. Quiet hillside alternative to dense Batumi towers is a plausible offer.
  • Proof stack is weak. Little visible evidence of current site reality, live amenities, or successful operations.
  • Too much portal dependency. The project does not dominate its own search footprint.
  • Location language is soft. “10–15 minutes” and “5 minutes to sea” undersell actual access friction.

Sales failure

The public pitch is stronger than the public evidence.
  • Investor language is aggressive. Installments, bank comfort, fast payback, management ease.
  • But buyers ask basic questions first. Is it finished, occupied, rentable, and actually functioning?
  • Resale leakage complicates the story. Secondary-market inventory starts to narrate the project instead of the developer.
  • Conversion likely collapses at diligence. Prospects get intrigued, then confused, then suspicious.

Consultant posture
Recommended posture

Be direct, document-focused, and hard to charm. Do not argue theory; ask for dated proof, exact numbers, and operational facts. If answers stay vague, treat that as the answer.

Primary diagnosis to test

Is this mainly a communications failure layered over a real asset — or a deeper completion / capital / execution problem that better marketing cannot mask?

  1. What is the exact current status today? Completed, partially completed, stalled, or relaunching — with a phase-by-phase breakdown, not sales language.
  2. Why do public sources conflict on completion timing? GEOLN shows Dec 2019, while Korter’s last tracking appears around Feb 2021. Which record is true?
  3. How many total units actually exist? Explain the 100-unit vs 300-apartment discrepancy and provide the definitive product map.
  4. What finish standard was actually delivered? Black frame, white frame, or turnkey — broken down by unit type.
  5. Which amenities are physically operational right now? Pool, spa, restaurant, shop, security, concierge, playground — live today or still planned?
  6. Why does the project still rely on stale aggregator pages? If the project is healthy, why is there no dominant official source of truth?
  7. Where is the current proof? Dated walkthroughs, live occupancy, resident activity, operator details, and current rent/use evidence.
  8. Why have contradictory public claims been left uncorrected for years? Is this neglect, or is there no internally agreed version of reality?
  9. Do the ROI claims still stand? If 4–5 year payback and 22% annual ROI were real, show actual operating numbers — not brochures.
  10. What is the 12-month recovery plan? Construction, operations, sales, communications, milestones, and who is accountable for each.
